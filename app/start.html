What the Safety Browser protects you from

These protections run automatically. There is nothing to set up.

Fake website addresses

Spots look-alike addresses such as paypa1.com, amaz0n-billing.com or a fake irs-refund site, plus hidden-letter and "@" tricks.

You'll see: a stop screen that names the real site, before the fake one loads.

Known phishing sites

Every address is checked against a list of active phishing sites from OpenPhish, refreshed every 6 hours. The check happens on your computer, so nobody sees where you browse.

You'll see: "on a list of known phishing sites" and a stop screen.

Fake virus warnings

Catches "your computer is locked, call Microsoft" pages. Stops them from taking over your screen, silences endless pop-ups and mutes alarm sounds.

You'll see: "Fake virus warning. Do not call."

Remote-control programs

Holds downloads of AnyDesk, TeamViewer, ScreenConnect and about 20 similar tools that scammers use to take over computers and bank accounts.

You'll see: a warning that needs "I KNOW THIS PERSON" typed to continue.

Risky program downloads

Pauses any program file (.exe, .msi and similar) so a fake "update" or "video player" can't slip onto your computer.

You'll see: a check that needs "INSTALL" typed to continue.

Payment pressure

Notices when a page pushes gift cards, crypto, Bitcoin ATMs or wire transfers together with threats or deadlines like "final notice" or "arrest".

You'll see: a yellow banner, or a stop screen when the pressure is strong.

Password & card guard

Warns you before you type a password, card number, bank details or Social Security number on a page that isn't encrypted or shows warning signs.

You'll see: "Stop before you type" above the page.

Brand-new websites

Checks how old a website is. Fake shops and copied sign-in pages are usually only days old and disappear soon after.

You'll see: "This website was created 4 days ago."

Your trusted sites

Sites you save in your Favorites are marked as trusted. Open your bank from there, not from emails, texts or search ads.

You'll see: "This site is in your Favorites."

Get Me Out
Feeling rushed, scared or unsure? Press the red Get Me Out button.
It's at the top right of the toolbar, or press Ctrl+Shift+X. It closes the page, stops any sound and brings you back here. Nothing on that page can stop it.

Good habits

Six rules that stop most scams

Software catches a lot. These habits catch the rest, including scams that arrive by phone or text.

  1. Real companies never ask for gift cards, crypto or wire transfers. Not the IRS, Medicare, your bank or the police.
  2. Pop-ups with phone numbers are always fake. Microsoft and Apple don't put phone numbers in warnings. Don't call.
  3. Never let a stranger control your computer. If a caller asks you to install a program, hang up.
  4. Go to your bank yourself. Use your Favorites or type the address. Don't use links in emails, texts or ads.
  5. Rushing means stop. "Act now" and "don't tell anyone" are scam signs. Hang up and call someone you trust.
  6. Have a family safe word. AI can fake a loved one's voice. Ask for the word before sending money in an "emergency". Keep it in Notes.
